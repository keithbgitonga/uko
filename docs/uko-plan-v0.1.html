01Executive summary

Uko? — Swahili for “Where are you?” — is a rider-tracking and scheduled-rides platform. A rider books now or for later, a driver-partner sees exactly where they are needed and when, and an operator sees every ride, rider and shift on one live map.

Bolt's rider and driver apps are the reference baseline. They are the region's benchmark for on-demand booking and, importantly, they already treat scheduled rides as a first-class feature on both sides of the marketplace. We will study both Android builds — statically and evidence-first — to recover the observable product contract: screens, flows, state machines, integration points and scheduling rules. Those findings become weighted requirements for Uko?; they are not code to copy.

This document is Step 0: the plan, not the findings. It defines what Uko? should do, what we will inspect in Bolt, how the system is built, the order of delivery and the decisions needed from you before Step 1.

02What Uko? is

One-line definition. Uko? lets a passenger see and schedule a ride in seconds, lets a driver-partner see exactly where they are needed and when, and lets an operator see every ride, rider and shift on one live map — with a shareable tracking link for the people waiting at home.

Who it serves
UserJob to be doneWhat Uko? gives them
Passenger“I need a ride now, or at 7am tomorrow — and I want to know where it is.”On-demand booking with upfront price; scheduled booking; live driver location and ETA; share-trip link; M-Pesa, card or cash; receipts and support.
Driver-partner“I want predictable work, clear rules and visible earnings.”On-demand offers; scheduled job list; navigation and trip flow; availability planner; earnings and commission breakdown; ratings and incentives.
Operator / dispatcher“I need every ride and rider accounted for, and I need to fix exceptions fast.”Live map; schedule board with assignment; driver roster and documents; cancellation and no-show handling; payouts and reconciliation.
Family / trusted contact“I just want to know they got there safely.”No-install track link with live location and ETA; arrival notification; optional SMS fallback.
Positioning: four claims we must earn
Certainty
Scheduled, not hoped-for
A booking is confirmed with a driver, a reminder and a fallback — not a request fired into the void at 7am.
Visibility
Live at every step
Rider, driver and operator see the same state: searching, assigned, arriving, in ride, complete.
Control
Ops in the loop
Dispatchers can assign, reassign, call, refund and spot a late ride before the rider complains.
Local-first
Built for Kenya
M-Pesa and cash, low-bandwidth behaviour, English and Swahili, and Android devices that kill background apps.
Scope assumptions to confirm
  • A1 · Market: Kenya, pilot in one Nairobi zone; pricing, regulation and payments assume Kenya.
  • A2 · “Rider” means both sides: the passenger who rides and the rider-partner (driver) who provides it. Passengers book ahead; operators schedule rider-partners.
  • A3 · Platform: Android-first rider and driver apps; responsive web dispatch console; iOS after the pilot.
  • A4 · Vehicle types at MVP: standard car and boda-boda. Premium, XL and shuttle are configuration, not new builds.
  • A5 · Brand: “Uko?” is a working title; identity, logo and voice will be original and must not reuse Bolt assets.

The scheduled-ride rule catalogue — assignment, online-by, wait timers, cancellation windows and prepayment — is on page 7. Features vary by country and app version.

Scheduled-ride rules visible in Bolt's driver guidance — to be verified in Phase 1
  • Drivers accept scheduled requests in advance; each shows a fixed price and an “online by” time.
  • While a scheduled ride is accepted, the driver is offered only non-overlapping, nearby requests.
  • The wait clock starts at the scheduled start time; the rider grace period is 5 minutes.
  • Rider no-show: wait 10 minutes, then the driver cancels and the fee is applied automatically.
  • Rider cancels less than 1 hour before pickup → cancellation fee.
  • Cash is not available for scheduled rides; payment is in-app only.
04Reverse-engineering plan: Bolt Rider + Bolt Driver

Objective. Recover the observable product contract of both apps — screens, flows, state machines, integration points, scheduling rules and edge-case handling — as evidence-backed requirements for Uko?. Every claim carries an evidence reference and a confidence label (observed, derived or inferred). We do not copy code, assets, protected text or visual design. Estimate: 8–10 working days for both builds.

What we will inspect
  • Package & manifest — permissions, services, receivers, deep links, exported components, SDK inventory.
  • Build, signing & code map — split APKs/ABIs, SDK levels, certificate, obfuscation, integrity checks, package structure, feature flags.
  • Identity & auth — OTP, token lifecycle, device binding, logout/deletion, permissions.
  • Network contract — hosts, REST/GraphQL/protobuf, realtime channel, retries/offline, certificate pinning.
  • Ride domain — booking, matching, driver state machine, cancellation reasons, fare breakdown, receipts.
  • Scheduling domain — entry points, date/time rules, advance assignment, reminders, cancellation windows, prepayment.
  • Tracking & location — cadence, background service behaviour, battery settings, map/navigation SDK, geofences.
  • Safety & notifications — SOS, share trip, ride check, masked calls, push channels, deep links.
  • Driver economics — earnings, commission, incentives, cash debt, statements.

Guardrails
  • Analyse only lawfully obtained, publicly available builds and documentation.
  • No authentication bypass, DRM circumvention, anti-tamper defeat or live user-data interception.
  • No Bolt code, assets, strings, logos or UI lifted into Uko?; implementation is clean-room from requirements.
  • Server-side logic is never recoverable from an APK. Gaps become documented unknowns and Uko? product decisions — never guesses presented as Bolt behaviour.
  • Legal and IP review before Phase 1 begins.
Tools & evidence standard
  • Craft Anything and REA for structured, evidence-ID-backed Android analysis; jadx and apktool for decompilation.
  • Emulator and proxy capture only for authorised runtime checks on test accounts.
  • Unknowns are logged explicitly — a bounded search is never reported as proof of absence.

Exit gate: the parity matrix is reviewed and the MVP scope is frozen. Deliverables: feature/flow report, weighted parity matrix, state diagrams and an unknowns log.

UX principles
PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Fast and forgivingThree taps to book; scheduling is a toggle on fare-confirm, not a separate journey. Optimistic UI, queued actions and SMS fallback make weak 3G survivable.
Trust by designFare, cancellation terms and payment method visible before confirm; receipts show the full breakdown; SOS and share-trip are always one tap away.

Design intent. Calm, locally grounded and visibly its own product — deep green/navy, warm amber for action, generous type and a map that stays legible in sunlight. No Bolt colours, iconography or screen patterns.

06The scheduling engine and ride lifecycle

Scheduling is the feature that makes Uko? more than a Bolt clone. Bolt already treats advance rides as first-class on both sides; Uko? adds operator scheduling, conflict-aware assignment and explicit on-time accountability.

Scheduling rules for the MVP
RuleBehaviour
Advance windowBook from 30 minutes to 90 days ahead. Requests closer than the lead time are routed to on-demand matching instead.
Lead timeConfigurable per zone; default 45 minutes for automatic assignment, 30 minutes for operator-created bookings.
AssignmentPre-assign at T-24h where supply allows; otherwise broadcast at T-45m; re-broadcast at T-15m if unassigned; dispatcher override always available.
Driver online-byDriver reminder at T-60m; must be online and en route by T-20m; if not, the ride is reassigned and the driver's reliability score is affected.
Conflict checkA driver cannot hold overlapping scheduled rides or shifts. A zone-configurable travel buffer (15–30 minutes) is enforced between jobs.
PaymentScheduled rides are prepaid: M-Pesa STK push at booking or card authorisation. Cash is disabled for scheduled rides.
CancellationRider cancels free up to 2 hours before pickup; a fee applies inside 2 hours. Driver cancellation is penalised above a threshold. No-show: wait 10 minutes, then cancel with fee.
RemindersRider: T-24h and T-1h. Driver: job accepted, T-60m and T-20m. Both receive “driver assigned” and “driver en route”.
Accountability95% of scheduled rides assigned at least 1 hour before pickup; on-time pickup ≥ 90% during the pilot; every miss has a reason code.

Edge cases the engine must handle
  • Driver goes offline or loses signal after accepting a scheduled job.
  • Vehicle breakdown or document expiry discovered before pickup.
  • Rider is late; wait timer and no-show fee start at the scheduled time.
  • Two bookings for the same rider at the same time (duplicate guard).
  • Payment authorisation fails at activation; grace period then cancel.
  • App killed by the OS mid-trip; server reconciles from last event.
  • Dispatch reassigns a ride while the driver is en route.
  • Zone or fare-rule change between booking and pickup (price is fixed at booking).
